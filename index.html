CASE STUDY / 01

Making onboarding more relevant to each customer

The challenge: The existing onboarding process followed a one size fits all model. Every customer received an initial onboarding call followed by follow up calls. A small customer with five users and an Enterprise customer with more than twenty users had very different needs, but the experience did not reflect those differences. Within each account, users also needed different things from the product.

My approach: I created a central onboarding repository with supporting materials and Loom videos so customers could learn at their own pace. I moved away from treating the account as one audience. Instead, I considered what each user needed to achieve with the product, grouped users around the value they were looking for and adapted the guidance to those needs. This made onboarding more relevant to the person using the product, as well as to the size and complexity of the account.

Product collaboration: I worked with the Engineering team to explain why different users needed different views of the product. This contributed to the introduction of pinned views, allowing each user to pin and update the information relevant to their work without being distracted by other users’ activity.

Implementation: The onboarding journey typically took four to six weeks. The product update took more than a quarter to deliver, so the repository and learning materials helped customers make progress while the product enhancement was being developed.

Outcome: A more personalised onboarding experience and faster progress towards first value. Customer satisfaction increased by 23%, measured through customer surveys and feedback scores. The tailored approach to individual user needs contributed to a 40% improvement in product adoption, with training materials and walkthroughs providing additional support.
CASE STUDY / 02

Helping customers see the full value of the product

The challenge: Downloadable files were introduced as a quick way for busy customers to access summary information. Some customers began relying on these files instead of returning to the platform. They missed ongoing updates and other capabilities available through the product, leading to reduced usage.

What I identified: The adoption gap was largely an awareness issue. Customers did not fully understand the difference between a static download and the continuously updated experience available on the platform.

My approach: I sent informative emails, shared training videos, conducted setup calls and delivered product walkthroughs. I also organised a session with the Head of Product and Engineering so customers could hear directly about the platform and its capabilities.

Customer engagement: These touchpoints helped customers understand the broader value of the platform and gave them opportunities to ask questions and get practical guidance on using it in their regular workflow.

Outcome: Product usage increased and customer engagement improved. Customers developed a better understanding of the ongoing value of the platform, which supported retention. This education work reinforced the wider effort to help customers understand the product and use more of its capabilities. The 40% adoption improvement is attributed to the broader approach of understanding and catering to individual user needs, supported by training and walkthroughs.
CASE STUDY / 03

Building strategic relationships across customer accounts

The challenge: Enterprise and SMB customers needed different engagement strategies. Enterprise relationships often began with Directors who identified the right users. SMB relationships involved CEOs, CROs, VPs and department heads alongside the people using the product day to day.

My approach: I adapted communication to the account and the people involved. For Enterprise customers, I typically shared weekly updates with Directors during onboarding, then moved to twice monthly or monthly updates depending on the number of users and account activity. For SMB customers, I maintained twice monthly updates with senior leaders such as CEOs and department heads while staying connected to the people using the product day to day.

Building multiple champions: I work to develop relationships with multiple stakeholders in each organisation. This reduces dependency on one contact, creates continuity when a champion changes roles and can open a relationship with a new organisation when an existing champion moves companies.

Internal collaboration: I focus on clear handovers from Sales, realistic timelines with Product and Engineering, understanding team capacity and communicating expectations honestly. Customer feedback helps guide what we prioritise and how we respond.

Outcome: A more structured account engagement model, stronger stakeholder relationships and better coordination between customers and internal teams.
CASE STUDY / 04

Rebuilding trust and retaining an at risk customer

The challenge: An SMB customer became quiet after its department head moved to another company. Although an introduction had been made to the new contact, the new stakeholder was reluctant to adopt the product into their existing workflow. Calls were repeatedly postponed and participation declined.

My approach: Rather than continuing to request meetings, I prepared an informative account summary showing how the company had benefited from the product. I then shared a focused preview of how the new stakeholder could use the platform to speed up specific aspects of their own work.

Rebuilding the relationship: I focused on demonstrating practical value and making the communication relevant to the new stakeholder, rather than simply asking them to use the product. This helped me build trust and restart the conversation.

Outcome: The customer continued using the product and renewed its subscription. I helped rebuild engagement with an account that had become difficult to reach.
